Investment conclusion. Quickmart's operating history and cash generation justify serious consideration. However, the proposed price is not automatically attractive once lease principal, reinvestment and an equity cost of capital are considered. The scenario outputs below are illustrative analyst assumptions—not market quotes, independently verified forecasts or an audited valuation. A final subscription mandate requires model and source verification.

1. Executive investment thesis and variant perception

Quickmart has expanded revenue from KES 25.68bn in 2021 to KES 50.43bn in 2025. Its grocery footprint, supplier terms and operating leverage are genuine positives. The market's headline EV/adjusted EBITDA comparison can overstate cheapness if IFRS 16 lease effects are ignored. The investor must also distinguish reported from adjusted earnings and recognise that this IPO injects no capital into Quickmart.

5. Cash-flow valuation — primary method

Approach: cash-to-equity DCF = operating cash flow after tax less cash capex and lease principal repayments, discounted at an assumed KES cost of equity. This is an FCFE-style valuation, not the enterprise FCFF/WACC model originally requested. It avoids a direct IFRS 16 lease-liability deduction after already charging cash lease principal. It assumes net conventional borrowing and other financing flows are immaterial or sustainable at neutral levels; this must be validated. For a formal FCFF/WACC cross-check, rebuild cash operating costs and exclude lease principal consistently before deducting lease liabilities in the EV-to-equity bridge.

8. Earnings quality, leases and governance

  • FY2026E adjusted EBITDA of KES 6.585bn exceeds reported EBITDA of KES 6.279bn by KES 306m; review the related-party and other cost add-backs.
  • As of H1 2026, the issuer's net debt including lease liabilities was approximately KES 7.273bn. A post-IFRS 16 EBITDA multiple must be compared against enterprise values with compatible lease treatment.
  • FY2025 dividends of approximately KES 1.647bn exceeded PAT of KES 1.509bn; management's FY2026–27 dividend forecasts also imply high payout ratios.
  • Information Memorandum describes consultancy/management agreements with promoter-linked entities terminated effective July 2026; review residual obligations, lease arrangements and governance safeguards.
  • H1 2026 cash flow should be reconciled for reclassification of KES 1.1bn shareholder advance related to dividends.

Pre-mortem: A 40% loss could arise from weak store productivity, margin compression, supplier-credit reversal, lease-driven fixed costs or post-listing selling pressure. Each is testable through quarterly disclosures.

10. Decision framework and what changes our mind

Recommendation: WATCH / DEFER pending a formal independent FCFF/WACC model, confirmation of debt and lease schedules, forecast cash-flow reconciliation and comparison against contemporaneous Kenyan government yields. A cash-to-equity model is provided here as an initial intrinsic-value discipline, but should not be the sole basis of a subscription.

Upgrade triggers: independently confirmed cash after lease repayments and growth capex, credible store-level incremental ROIC, and governance protections. Downgrade triggers: supplier-term deterioration, persistent distributions above sustainable free cash flow, or growth that dilutes returns.
